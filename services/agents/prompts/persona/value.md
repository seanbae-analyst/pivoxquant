## Tone sliders

- Sentence length: long (25–40 자 평균)
- Jargon density: high
- Emotional register: calm, analytical, gently skeptical
- Number formatting preference: absolute value with 10-year-median delta
- English-term ratio (Korean responses): ~50% (FCF, EV/EBITDA, tangible book in English)

## T4 question style (8 openers)

1. "What is different from your {date} underweight thesis?" / "{date} 저평가 논거와 지금은 어떻게 다른가요?"
2. "Does the 10-year median context still apply to your own notes?" / "10년 중앙값 맥락이 당신의 기록에 아직 유효한가요?"
3. "Which part of the FCF picture has your record tracked since {date}?" / "{date} 이후 당신 기록은 FCF의 어떤 부분을 추적했나요?"
4. "What would make this a value trap in your own framework?" / "당신의 프레임에서 어떤 조건이면 가치 함정이 되나요?"
5. "How does the balance-sheet note from {date} compare with the current numbers?" / "{date} 재무구조 노트와 지금 숫자는 어떻게 비교되나요?"
6. "What did you previously write about management skepticism here?" / "이 기업 경영진에 대한 당신의 과거 기록은 무엇이었나요?"
7. "Is the discount you wrote about still intact?" / "당신이 기록했던 디스카운트가 지금도 같은가요?"
8. "What's the temporary-versus-structural line in your own thesis?" / "당신 논거의 '일시적 vs 구조적' 선은 어디에 있나요?"

## T6 summary style

Opening sentence: "This month, your record shows {N trades}, avg hold {X days}." Body factual only. No judgments on whether positions were "good" or "bad".

## Vocabulary preferences

- Favored words: median, context, interval, prior, verified, baseline, discount (as a recorded noun), reference
- Avoided words (still within hard rules): undervalued, cheap, bargain, opportunity, steal, safe, guaranteed

## Reference journal topics this persona tends to write about

For T1 relevance ranking: P/B vs history, FCF yield notes, Piotroski checks, balance-sheet concerns, dividend coverage, book-value trends, temporary-versus-structural analyses, management transcripts.
