## 1. How does Gartner define "AI-ready data", and what are its headline predictions and 2025-2026 statements?

### Takeaway
Gartner defines AI-ready data contextually: data is ready only relative to a specific AI use case and technique, must be representative of that use case (including errors and outliers), and readiness is demonstrated through metadata that lets the organization align, qualify and govern the data as a continuous practice. Its headline forecast (published 2025-02-26) is conditional: 60% of AI projects *that are not supported by AI-ready data* will be abandoned through 2026; it is not a claim about all AI projects, and it is a prediction, not a measured result.

### Cited Findings

**Definition and framing**
- Gartner's Q&A with analyst Roxane Edjlali (press release dated 2025-02-26) says AI-ready data "must be representative of the use case", including the patterns, errors, outliers and unexpected emergence needed to train or run the model, and that readiness is shown through metadata that aligns, qualifies and governs the data. [search extract of the primary; same text confirmed in a fetched reprint] — [Gartner press release, 2025-02-26](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-26-lack-of-ai-ready-data-puts-ai-projects-at-risk); [Technology Decisions reprint by Edjlali, 2025-05-27](https://www.technologydecisions.com.au/content/it-management/article/how-to-prepare-your-data-for-ai-success-14279115)

### Inferences
- Gartner now has at least three different "60%" forecasts in circulation (60% of AI projects without AI-ready data abandoned through 2026; 60% failing to govern AI by 2027 because of culture; 60% failing to realize AI value by 2027 because governance is not integrated). They are easy to conflate; the report should quote each with its condition and date.
- Gartner's message shifted between early 2025 and late 2026 from "data must be AI-ready" (metadata, pipelines, observability) toward governance, culture and investment in foundations. The definition itself (use-case-specific, continuous) did not change.
- As of 2026-11-25 the "through 2026" forecast window is about to close. I found no Gartner follow-up that measures whether 60% of such projects were actually abandoned, so the figure remains a forecast.
- "Fit for a specific AI use case" is an accurate paraphrase of Gartner's position, but I did not find that exact phrase in a Gartner primary; the closest primary wording is "representative of the use case" and "determined contextually to the use case and AI technique".

## 2. How do other bodies define it (ESIP/NSF/NCAR, US Commerce, OECD, FAIR-R / FAIR for AI, ISO/IEC 5259, NIST, ODI, MLCommons Croissant)?

### Takeaway
There is no single standard definition. Public-sector and community bodies define AI-readiness as a set of dataset, metadata, access and governance properties for *publishers* (ESIP checklist 2023, US Commerce guidelines January 2025, ODI framework 2025 with v2 in December 2025, UK government guidelines January 2026), while ISO/IEC 5259 standardizes data quality for analytics and ML without using "AI-ready" as its organizing term, and Croissant standardizes the machine-readable metadata layer. I found no formal NIST or OECD definition of the term.

### Cited Findings

**ESIP (Earth Science Information Partners) checklist; NSF; NCAR**
- "Checklist to Examine AI-readiness for Open Environmental Datasets" v1.0 is maintained by the ESIP Data Readiness Cluster (chair: Douglas Rao), with members from NOAA, NASA, USGS and others. The GitHub repo lists v1.0 as last updated 2023-12-20; the PolarWatch copy's history says 2023-06-21 (page header "Updated 04/03/2024"). A beta was released in 2022. **[fetched]** — [ESIP data-readiness repo](https://github.com/esipfed/data-readiness); [PolarWatch copy of checklist v1.0](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html); [ESIP mailing list, 2022 beta](https://lists.esipfed.org/pipermail/esip-datareadiness/2022/000054.html)
- Origin: builds on a 2019 draft data-readiness matrix from the White House OSTP Subcommittee on Open Science. It gives no one-sentence definition; it assesses how far a dataset meets data management practices relevant to AI/ML, ideally at the data-file / single-variable level. **[fetched]** — [PolarWatch copy](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html)
- Checklist sections: General information; Data quality (timeliness, completeness, consistency, bias, plus provenance and integrity checks); Data documentation (community-standard machine-readable metadata, data dictionary, persistent identifier, ontology crosswalks, example code, license, prior AI/ML use); Data access (open machine-readable formats, API/cloud delivery, handling of restricted data); Data preparation (null/gap filling, outliers, gridding, original-resolution availability, suitability as labels). **[fetched]** — [PolarWatch copy](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html)
- NSF: an NSF workshop report (arXiv, December 2024) says there is still insufficient understanding of the criteria for AI-ready data, suggests degrees of readiness may be useful, and proposes candidate criteria such as standard representations and verified integrity and quality; these are candidates, not an adopted definition. [search extract] — [Envisioning National Resources for AI Research: NSF Workshop Report, arXiv 2412.10278](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10278)
- NCAR: I found no NCAR-authored AI-readiness checklist. NCAR appears in NOAA's AI-ready data page only as the provider of the AI4ESS summer school. [search extract] — [NOAA AI-Ready Data page](https://noaa.gov/node/7996)

**US Department of Commerce (January 2025)**
- "Generative Artificial Intelligence and Open Data: Guidelines and Best Practices", Version 1, published 2025-01-16 by the AI and Open Government Data Assets Working Group under the Commerce Data Governance Board (group formed 2023). Written for Commerce bureaus but published for data publishers globally; described as a dynamic resource to be revised. **[fetched coverage]** — [Benton summary, 2025-01-16](https://www.benton.org/headlines/generative-artificial-intelligence-and-open-data-guidelines-and-best-practices); [Commerce PDF (primary, not fetchable here)](https://www.commerce.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/GenerativeAI-Open-Data.pdf)
- Five guideline categories: documentation; formats of data and metadata; storage and dissemination; licensing and usage; data integrity and quality. Example recommendations: comprehensive variable-level metadata, easier bulk download/compression, regularly updated data websites, machine-readable usage policies, automated AI-ready data quality control. **[fetched coverage]** — [FedScoop, 2025-01-16](https://fedscoop.com/commerce-releases-guideance-generative-ai-data/)
- Framing quote from then Chief Data Officer Oliver Wise: "machine-readable alone isn't sufficient to meet the needs of those new tools" and "we must embrace practices that make data not only machine-readable but machine-understandable". **[fetched coverage]** — [FedScoop, 2025-01-16](https://fedscoop.com/commerce-releases-guideance-generative-ai-data/)
- The document is reported as 79 pages. A September 2025 Commerce AI strategy plan (for OMB M-25-21) lists supporting open data structured for generative AI use as a commitment. [search extract] — [DOC AI Strategy Plan, 2025-09](https://www.commerce.gov/sites/default/files/2025-09/DOC-AI-Strategy-Plan-for-OMB-Memorandum-M-25-21.pdf)

**Open Data Institute (ODI), UK**
- ODI published "A framework for AI-ready data" in 2025, built from a literature review, expert interviews and ODI experience; criteria target "data, metadata, and infrastructure design". The page states it was "updated with v2 of the framework in December 2025" and calls itself a first iteration. **[fetched]** — [ODI framework page](https://theodi.org/insights/reports/a-framework-for-ai-ready-data/)
- ODI's working definition: "AI-ready data is data designed with transparency and technical usability for AI practitioners in mind." **[fetched]** — [ODI blog, "How ready is open data for AI?" (2025)](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/how-ready-is-open-data-for-ai/)
- Dimensions as of v2: (1) dataset properties (standards conformance, consistent semantics, imbalances, de-identification, suitable file formats); (2) metadata (machine-readable, documentation, technical specs, supply-chain/provenance, legal clarity); (3) surrounding infrastructure (portals, APIs, version control); (4) governance, added in the update (policy-as-code, documented roles, public contact points, clear access processes). **[fetched]** — [ODI blog (2025)](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/how-ready-is-open-data-for-ai/); [ODI governance update](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/bringing-governance-into-the-odi-framework-for-ai-ready-data/)
- ODI assessed 10 open datasets from its Open Data Use Case Observatory: some lack APIs; many lack ML-specific metadata; only one (CommonCorpus) implemented Croissant; uncertainty is rarely documented (Google Open Buildings an exception); few have full version control; governance is mostly human-readable rather than machine-executable. Conclusion: "simply making open datasets available online is not enough". **[fetched]** — [ODI blog (2025)](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/how-ready-is-open-data-for-ai/)
- A peer-reviewed write-up (Majithia, Carey-Wilson, Simperl, Shadbolt, *AI Magazine*, 2026, DOI 10.1002/aaai.70054) presents the framework and argues existing approaches (FAIR, ESIP checklist) give assessment dimensions but not actionable, holistic publisher guidance; it calls FAIR too broad for this purpose. [search extract] — [Oxford ORA record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:a0bce89b-3597-4a20-a0aa-65c177a47149)

**UK government (January 2026)**
- "Guidelines and best practices for making government datasets ready for AI", published on GOV.UK on 2026-01-19 (Government Digital Service / DSIT). **MLCommons Croissant**
- Croissant is a metadata format for ML-ready datasets developed under MLCommons; v1.0 was announced March 2024 with a specification, example datasets, a Python library and a visual editor. It builds on schema.org and does not change the data's representation, only describes it. At launch Kaggle, Hugging Face and OpenML supported it, and TensorFlow, PyTorch and JAX could load Croissant datasets via TensorFlow Datasets; the paper says it spans hundreds of thousands of datasets. [search extract] — [Croissant paper, arXiv 2403.19546](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19546v3); [Google Research blog, 2024-03](https://research.google/blog/croissant-a-metadata-format-for-ml-ready-datasets/?m=1)

### Inferences
- The definitions split along the publisher/consumer line. Gartner defines readiness from the consuming enterprise's side (fit to my use case). Commerce, ODI, ESIP and the UK guidance define it from the publishing side (properties a dataset should have so unknown AI users can consume it). The UK text is the only public-sector one I found that explicitly adopts Gartner-style use-case dependence.
- Government definitions converged between 2025 and 2026 on roughly four layers: the data itself, metadata/documentation, access infrastructure, and governance/legal. ODI's December 2025 addition of governance and the UK's January 2026 pillars are evidence of that convergence.
- ISO/IEC 5259 is the nearest thing to a formal standard, but it standardizes "data quality for analytics and ML", which is narrower than what the frameworks above call AI-readiness (it does not cover API access, licensing clarity or discoverability metadata to the same degree). This is my reading of the part titles and abstracts, not of the full standards.

- US Commerce PDF itself (79 pages) could not be fetched (403). Whether it contains a formal one-line definition, and whether it names Croissant, schema.org, DCAT or knowledge graphs, is unverified here. Whether a Version 2 has been issued since January 2025 is also unverified.

- ODI framework: original publication month in 2025 and the full criterion list were not on the fetched page (they are in linked PDFs). Sources also describe the structure differently (three categories in v1, four dimensions in v2, "four components" in the UK paraphrase); the v2 PDF should be the reference.

## 3. What are the commonly listed requirements that make data "AI-ready"?

### Takeaway
Across Gartner, ESIP, US Commerce, ODI and the UK guidance, the recurring requirements are: rich machine-readable metadata and semantics, documented provenance/lineage, quality that is measured against the intended use (including bias and representativeness), clear licensing and access governance, timeliness/versioning, open machine-readable formats with API access, and human-plus-machine documentation. Metadata is the single most consistently emphasized item.

### Cited Findings
- Metadata and semantics: Gartner makes metadata the mechanism by which readiness is proven (align, qualify, govern) and calls for moving from passive to active metadata. — [Technology Decisions reprint of Gartner, 2025-05-27](https://www.technologydecisions.com.au/content/it-management/article/how-to-prepare-your-data-for-ai-success-14279115). Commerce calls for comprehensive variable-level metadata so data is "machine-understandable". — [FedScoop, 2025-01-16](https://fedscoop.com/commerce-releases-guideance-generative-ai-data/). ESIP asks for community-standard, machine-readable metadata, a data dictionary and ontology crosswalks. — [ESIP checklist v1.0](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html). ODI lists machine-readable metadata, technical specifications and consistent meaning of terms. — [ODI blog (2025)](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/how-ready-is-open-data-for-ai/)
- Lineage / provenance: ESIP includes provenance tracking and integrity checks under quality. — [ESIP checklist v1.0](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html). ODI asks for supply-chain information in metadata. — [ODI blog (2025)](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/how-ready-is-open-data-for-ai/). Gartner's Chandrasekaran names data lineage and classification as one of three pillars. — [TechTarget, 2026-03-13](https://www.techtarget.com/searchenterpriseai/feature/AI-ready-data-needs-its-own-set-of-rules-experts-say)
- Quality fit-for-use: Gartner says the data must be representative of the use case, including errors and outliers, and that data with unresolved issues is not AI-ready. — [Technology Decisions, 2025-05-27](https://www.technologydecisions.com.au/content/it-management/article/how-to-prepare-your-data-for-ai-success-14279115). ESIP's quality dimensions: completeness, consistency, bias, uncertainty, timeliness, provenance, integrity. — [ESIP checklist v1.0](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html). ISO/IEC 5259-2 provides a data quality model and measurable characteristics for analytics and ML. — [ISO/IEC 5259-2](https://www.iso.org/standard/81860.html)
- Representativeness / bias: ESIP asks whether known bias is documented and mitigated. — [ESIP checklist v1.0](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html). ODI lists imbalances in the data under dataset properties and notes uncertainty is rarely documented. — [ODI blog (2025)](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/how-ready-is-open-data-for-ai/)
- Governance, access rights, licensing: Commerce has a dedicated "licensing and usage" guideline, including machine-readable usage policies. — [FedScoop, 2025-01-16](https://fedscoop.com/commerce-releases-guideance-generative-ai-data/). ESIP asks whether the license is machine-readable and how restricted data is handled (aggregation, de-identification, secure access). — [ESIP checklist v1.0](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html). ODI v2 adds governance (policy-as-code, roles, contact points, access processes) and legal clarity. — [ODI blog (2025)](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/how-ready-is-open-data-for-ai/). UK pillar 4 is legal, security and ethical compliance. — [GOV.UK, 2026-01-19](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-government-datasets-ready-for-ai/guidelines-and-best-practices-for-making-government-datasets-ready-for-ai). Gartner step 2 is defining governance requirements with legal and business leaders. — [Technology Decisions, 2025-05-27](https://www.technologydecisions.com.au/content/it-management/article/how-to-prepare-your-data-for-ai-success-14279115)
- Freshness / versioning: ESIP timeliness items (update plan, frequency, superseded versions). — [ESIP checklist v1.0](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html). ODI: version control needed to reproduce training; few datasets have it. — [ODI blog (2025)](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/how-ready-is-open-data-for-ai/). Gartner: pipelines for live feeds plus continuous monitoring; AI metadata used to detect drift. — [TechTarget, 2026-03-13](https://www.techtarget.com/searchenterpriseai/feature/AI-ready-data-needs-its-own-set-of-rules-experts-say)
- Machine-readable formats and access infrastructure: Commerce guidelines on formats and on storage/dissemination. — [FedScoop, 2025-01-16](https://fedscoop.com/commerce-releases-guideance-generative-ai-data/). ESIP: open non-proprietary formats, API/web service with open standard, cloud availability. — [ESIP checklist v1.0](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html). ODI: APIs matter in practice; the NHSBSA English Prescribing Data API handled about 6.3 million calls in 2023 versus roughly 300,000 manual downloads. — [ODI blog (2025)](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/how-ready-is-open-data-for-ai/)
- Documentation: Commerce's first guideline is documentation, for humans and machines. — [FedScoop, 2025-01-16](https://fedscoop.com/commerce-releases-guideance-generative-ai-data/). ESIP asks for example code/notebooks, intended-use recommendations and record of prior AI/ML use. — [ESIP checklist v1.0](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html). Croissant is the machine-readable carrier for this kind of dataset documentation. — [Croissant paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19546v3)
- Preparation for ML specifically: ESIP's data-preparation section (null handling, outliers, gridding, label suitability and labeling standards). — [ESIP checklist v1.0](https://polarwatch.github.io/datamanagement/guidelines/ai-ready-data-checklist-v.1.0.html)
- Organizational capability: UK pillar 3 (governance, collaboration, skills, documentation). — [GOV.UK, 2026-01-19](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-government-datasets-ready-for-ai/guidelines-and-best-practices-for-making-government-datasets-ready-for-ai). Gartner 2026: "AI-ready stakeholders". — [AIwire, 2026-09-21](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2026/09/21/gartner-predicts-ai-governance-failure-for-60-of-organizations-ignoring-data-culture-challenges/)

### Inferences
- A workable synthesis for the report is a five-layer list: (1) the data (quality, representativeness, format), (2) metadata and documentation, (3) provenance and versioning, (4) access infrastructure, (5) governance, rights and people. Every framework reviewed covers at least four of these.
- The frameworks disagree on outlier handling in a way worth flagging: ESIP's preparation section asks whether outliers were identified or removed, while Gartner says AI-ready data should *retain* the errors and outliers the use case needs. This is consistent with the use-case-dependence point rather than a contradiction, but it shows why a universal checklist is hard.

### Gaps
- Datasheets for Datasets / Data Cards / Dataset Nutrition Labels: none of the fetched frameworks' summaries named these explicitly. They are the standard academic documentation formats (Gebru et al.; Google Data Cards), but I did not retrieve a source in this session tying them to an "AI-ready data" framework, so that link is unsourced here.
- Requirements specific to unstructured data for RAG/agents (chunking, embeddings, access-control propagation) are discussed mostly by vendors; I did not find a standards-body treatment. Gartner's Chien mentions tagging and classifying unstructured data, with an unsourced "70%-90% of enterprise data is unstructured" claim ([TechTarget, 2026-03-13](https://www.techtarget.com/searchenterpriseai/feature/AI-ready-data-needs-its-own-set-of-rules-experts-say)).

## 4. How is AI-ready data different from "clean" or "analytics-ready" data, and what are the main criticisms?

### Takeaway
The claimed difference is that AI-readiness is relative to a specific use case and technique, is continuous, depends on context-carrying metadata, and can require keeping "messy" data that BI-style cleaning would remove. The main criticisms are that the term is undefined and untestable, that it relabels ordinary data management, that it blurs classic ML with LLM needs, and that in the open-data world it shifts cost onto publishers for the benefit of well-funded AI firms.

### Cited Findings

**Claimed differences**
- Gartner: traditional data management is "too slow, structured and rigid for AI teams"; AI-ready data must include the patterns, errors and outliers the model needs, rather than being scrubbed of them; readiness is ongoing. — [Technology Decisions reprint of Gartner, 2025-05-27](https://www.technologydecisions.com.au/content/it-management/article/how-to-prepare-your-data-for-ai-success-14279115)
- Gartner: readiness is per use case ("ready for one AI case but not for another"), and AI metadata is dynamic (drift detection) where traditional metadata is "often considered static". — [TechTarget, 2026-03-13](https://www.techtarget.com/searchenterpriseai/feature/AI-ready-data-needs-its-own-set-of-rules-experts-say)
- US Commerce: the shift is from machine-readable to machine-understandable. — [FedScoop, 2025-01-16](https://fedscoop.com/commerce-releases-guideance-generative-ai-data/)
- UK government: AI-readiness is "not defined solely by technical format" and cannot be reduced to a universal checklist. — [GOV.UK, 2026-01-19](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-government-datasets-ready-for-ai/guidelines-and-best-practices-for-making-government-datasets-ready-for-ai)
- ODI: publishing open data online is not enough; AI use needs APIs, ML metadata, uncertainty documentation and versioning beyond classic open-data practice. — [ODI blog (2025)](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/how-ready-is-open-data-for-ai/)

**Criticisms**
- Undefined / untestable: a Medium post on Snowflake's publication (vendor-affiliated) argues that without concrete criteria "AI-ready" becomes a buzzword, that Gartner's use-case-tied definition is not actionable, and notes that Databricks avoids defining the term. [search extract] — [Snowflake Medium post](https://medium.com/snowflake/what-does-ai-ready-data-mean-anyway-4dc103781be3)
- Relabeling of old work, and ML/LLM conflation: Sebastian Wernicke (2025-12-20) calls it "just a new label on old work"; argues classic ML needs clean structured tables while LLMs can use messy text, so the real LLM bottleneck is context (e.g. telling an active policy from an expired one) and the work shifts from cleansing to knowledge curation; says vendors cite ML requirements when challenged and LLM capabilities when selling. Opinion piece, no data. **[fetched]** — [Wernicke, "The 'AI-ready data' myth", 2025-12-20](https://datainspired.substack.com/p/the-ai-ready-data-myth)
- Binary framing: a podcast write-up with Profisee's chief data officer Malcolm Hawker (vendor executive) argues the phrase "flattens a spectrum into a binary" and that "ready for what?" is the right question. [search extract] — [TinyTechGuides](https://tinytechguides.com/blog/data-faces-malcolm-hawker-ep49-ai-ready-data/)
- Statistic built on an undefined term: one practitioner blog argues Gartner's 60% projection is of limited use because the phrase it depends on is rarely defined. Low-authority source. [search extract] — [Zyneto blog](https://zyneto.com/blog/ai-data-readiness)
- Open-data critique: Leigh Dodds (former ODI Director of Delivery), 2025-03-08, argues the FAIR-R paper never defines "Ready for AI", that most of it is already covered by FAIR, that the burden would fall on publishers while well-funded AI organisations could bear restructuring costs, that centring one type of use "increases the risk of eroding trust and creating harms", and that "we don't really need more principles"; he proposes a FAIR implementation profile for training datasets and better stewardship instead. **[fetched]** — [Leigh Dodds, 2025-03-08](https://blog.ldodds.com/2025/03/08/ai-ready-data-is-the-wrong-framing/)
- Academic critique of existing frameworks: the ODI/Oxford paper says FAIR is too broad and that FAIR-R and the ESIP checklist lack operational guidance. [search extract] — [Oxford ORA record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:a0bce89b-3597-4a20-a0aa-65c177a47149)
- Gartner's own caution against vendor claims: Edjlali warns a single-vendor solution "might not deliver on its promise"; Chien warns against labeling data AI-ready. — [TechTarget, 2026-03-13](https://www.techtarget.com/searchenterpriseai/feature/AI-ready-data-needs-its-own-set-of-rules-experts-say)

### Inferences
- The strongest proponents and the critics agree on use-case dependence. Gartner uses it as the definition; critics use it as evidence that a general "AI-ready" label is meaningless. The practical consequence is the same: assess readiness per use case, not per dataset or per company.
- The survey statistics in section 5 inherit this weakness. "Is your data AI-ready?" is self-assessed against no common definition, so differences between surveys (5-8% "fully ready" versus 32% "have a readiness process" versus 63% "lack or are unsure of practices") reflect question wording as much as reality.
- Most critiques found are blogs, podcasts or vendor-adjacent posts. The only peer-reviewed critique located is the ODI/Oxford AI Magazine article.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed empirical study was found testing whether "AI-ready" data practices (as defined by any framework) causally improve AI project outcomes.
- I did not find a formal vendor-neutral definition of "analytics-ready data" to contrast against; the contrast above is drawn from how AI-ready proponents characterize traditional practice.
