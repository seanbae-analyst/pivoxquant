# Korea's "AI-ready data" policy (2025-2026) and what it should mean for a one-person consumer product holding personal behavioural records

> Research date: 2026-11-25. Factual research, not legal advice.
> Reading guide for the report writer:
> - **[ENACTED]** = law/decree in force or formally resolved. **[GUIDELINE]** = non-binding official guidance. **[PLAN]** = announced intention. **[COMMENTARY]** = law-firm, press or practitioner interpretation.
> - Several official PDFs (data.go.kr guideline, PIPC guidebooks, law.go.kr statute text) could not be opened directly in this session. ## 1. Korean government: what has been announced about "AI-Ready 공공데이터", the AI 기본법 and standards?

### Takeaway
Korea's "AI-ready data" programme is a **public-sector open-data programme**: MOIS introduced the term in Sept 2025, the 공공데이터전략위원회 resolved a management plan plus a "TOP 100" list on 2026-01-06, and MOIS/NIA published a 70-page non-binding guideline (v1.0 dated 2026-03-31) whose content is essentially metadata, documentation, provenance/versioning, standard codes, six quality indicators and licence clarity. Nothing found imposes an "AI-ready data" duty on private companies; the AI 기본법 (in force 2026-01-22) touches data mainly as state promotion duties (학습용데이터 시책) plus a transparency notice duty for generative/high-impact AI.

### Cited Findings

**Timeline of the public-data programme**
- 2025-09-19 **[PLAN]**: MOIS announced it would introduce the "AI-Ready" concept to public data for the first time, defining AI-Ready 공공데이터 as public data refined/processed so AI can easily use it for learning, analysis and inference; planned criteria included attaching metadata (author, update cycle, change history), applying standard codes to identifiers such as addresses/administrative codes, and quality criteria; an expert working group was to produce detailed criteria and a management directive within 2025, applied first to high-AI-use datasets on the public data portal (from search-result summary of the article, not opened in full). — [서울신문 2025-09-19](https://m.go.seoul.co.kr/news/2025/09/19/20250919019003); government card-news on the same announcement: [korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr/multi/visualNewsView.do?newsId=148974512)
- 2025-11-27 **[PLAN]**: press headline that MOIS would open an AI-oriented public data "Top 100" in December (headline only; body not read). — [파이낸셜뉴스 2025-11-27](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202511271727593531)
- 2026-01-06 **[ENACTED as committee resolution]**: the 6th-term 공공데이터전략위원회 (2nd meeting) deliberated and resolved three items — "AI·고가치 공공데이터 TOP 100", an "AI-Ready 공공데이터 관리 방안", and the 5th 공공데이터 기본계획 (2026~2028). The plan: first distribute a guidebook containing the AI-Ready criteria and management method, **then amend the related directives**; apply the criteria first to 전국통합데이터 / 국가중점데이터 and other highly AI-usable or standardised data. TOP 100 spans 11 fields and is to be opened over three years. — [네이트 뉴스(통신사 전재) 2026-01-06](https://news.nate.com/view/20260106n26068) (page partly garbled when fetched; sequence and scope confirmed via search summary of sibling articles such as [this one](https://news.nate.com/view/20260106n26137))
- 2026-03-31 (issued) / 2026-04-24 (published) **[GUIDELINE]**: MOIS and NIA released 「공공데이터의 인공지능 친화적 관리 가이드라인 v1.0」 (70 pp., PDF + Markdown) addressed to public-institution data staff under the 공공데이터법. It states it is a **reference standard, not a legal obligation**, and will be revised as AI technology develops. **(secondary — community post that summarises the PDF with an LLM; original is on the public data portal)** — [PyTorch KR summary](https://discuss.pytorch.kr/t/v1-0-pdf-markdown-70p/10084); original: [data.go.kr 자료실](https://www.data.go.kr/bbs/rcr/selectRecsroom.do?pageIndex=1&originId=PDS_0000000001260+++&atchFileId=FILE_000000003631663)
- 2026-05-28 **[PLAN]**: at a 데이터 관계장관회의, MSIT, MOIS, MCST, PIPC and 국가데이터처 jointly announced the "AI 대전환 시대 데이터 정책 추진방향": open the "AI·고가치 공공데이터 탑 100"; convert unstructured public data (e.g. policy research reports) into AI-usable form; build reasoning (CoT) data, AI safety/trust datasets and benchmark data; pilot revenue-sharing "데이터 스페이스"; build a "범정부 데이터 파이프라인"; expand AI허브 into an "AI학습용데이터 통합제공시스템"; "AX 원스톱 바우처" for SMEs/startups; a tax credit for AI training-data purchases; a liability-exemption guide for public data officers. Legislative intentions: amend PIPA to allow **original (non-pseudonymised) data for public-interest AI training** subject to safeguards; amend the 공공데이터법 to give a basis for pseudonymising public data; a new 디지털헬스케어법; review of copyright law. The article reports no budget figure and does not use the phrase "AI-Ready". — [뉴시스 2026-05-28](https://mobile.newsis.com/view/NISX20260528_0003648050)
- 2026-07 **[GUIDELINE, unverified]**: a vendor blog (dated 2026-09-21, written by the CEO of a synthetic-data company and ending with a product pitch) says MOIS/NIA issued a **v1.1** in July 2026 with **15** management principles (v1.0 had 12), still voluntary, and that the 2026 public-data quality evaluation manual made the source DB of opened data a mandatory evaluation target. Treat as low-confidence until the primary text is checked. — [CUBIG blog 2026-09-21](https://cubig.ai/ko/articles/public-data-ai-friendly-guideline/)

**What the guideline actually defines (v1.0, secondary)** — all from [PyTorch KR summary](https://discuss.pytorch.kr/t/v1-0-pdf-markdown-70p/10084)
- Definition: AI-friendly public data = public data that secures **machine understandability and quality**, on the premise of minimising risk and protecting fundamental rights, so it can easily be used for AI training, inference and evaluation. Goal is "usable by AI without separate cleaning", versus the earlier goal of machine-readable formats.
- Four core properties: (1) machine-processable, interoperable structure — open formats, explicit schema, API, persistent identifiers and version info; (2) metadata and documentation — generation/cleaning process, annotation rules, quality-diagnosis method, recommended and non-recommended uses; (3) content quality and representativeness — balance, agreement with source, freshness, integrity, stated bias and limits; (4) rights/ethics/security/accountability governance — licence, personal-data handling, change-history tracking, misuse response and a contact point.
- Formats: recommended CSV, JSON, XML, Parquet, ORC (text/tabular); HWP/HWPX/DOC(X)/XLS(X)/PPT(X) are "not recommended" as the primary AI-facing format (not banned for originals or internal use).
- Value standards: dates ISO 8601, country ISO 3166-1, currency ISO 4217, language ISO 639-1, location WGS84/ISO 6709, SI units; missing values marked consistently (NULL / N/A) so they are distinguishable from errors.
- Metadata: based on W3C DCAT v3, Dublin Core and MLCommons Croissant; fields tiered required / recommended / optional. **Lineage metadata is required**: version, issued timestamp, modified timestamp, version notes describing concrete changes from the previous version. Usage metadata must state licence and whether **AI-training use is permitted**. Quality metadata records known biases, quality validation, known limitations and missing-data information.
- Documentation: a data card (modelled on Hugging Face dataset cards), schema definition / data dictionary, API document, quality-diagnosis report, personal-data/legal risk analysis, lineage and change-log documents.
- Six quality indicators for AI training: completeness, consistency, accuracy, timeliness, validity, uniqueness (incl. train/validation/test leakage and semantic duplicates). Suggested sharing minimum: missing-value ratio under 20%, all required metadata filled, no more than two missed update cycles, standard format.
- Governance: three data grades (open / restricted after de-identification / internal), retention tiers (5 / 3 / 1 years by purpose), version pinning; use types are view, analyse, combine, **AI training** — the last needing the data-owner institution's explicit consent and MOIS approval. Personal data follows the PIPC 가명정보 처리 가이드라인 procedure (preparation → risk review → pseudonymisation → adequacy review → safe management).
- Licence: a new 공공누리 "AI유형 (인공지능학습용)" permitting commercial use and derivative works without attribution.
- Stated limitation: criteria for unstructured data (structuring, text conversion, semantic chunking) are not yet sufficiently covered.
- References cited by the guideline: US Department of Commerce "Generative AI and Open Data" guidelines (2025), UK AI Opportunities Action Plan (2025), ODI "A Framework for AI-Ready Data" (2025), OECD Government at a Glance 2025.

**Existing legal basis for public-data quality**
- **[ENACTED]** MOIS runs public-data quality management under 공공데이터법 Article 22 (품질관리). — [행정안전부 공공데이터 품질관리 안내](https://mois.go.kr/frt/sub/a06/b02/openData_3/screen.do)

**AI 기본법 (인공지능 발전과 신뢰 기반 조성 등에 관한 기본법)**
- **[ENACTED]** In force **2026-01-22**. — [법률신문 해설](https://www.lawtimes.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=215801)
- **[ENACTED]/[COMMENTARY]** MSIT said fines (과태료) would be subject to a grace period of at least one year; commentators note the obligations themselves have applied since 2026-01-22 and the grace period is an enforcement-policy deferral. — [머니투데이 2025-12-31](https://www.mt.co.kr/tech/2025/12/31/2025123009504430416); [법률신문](https://www.lawtimes.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=215801)
- **[ENACTED] (secondary article map, data as of 2026-11-23)** Data-related provisions: Art. 2(12) defines 학습용데이터 as data used for developing/using AI; Art. 6(2)(4-2) puts creation/provision of public-data-based training data into the national basic plan; Art. 14 standardisation projects including training data (amended 2026-01-20); **Art. 15 — government policy and construction projects for training data** (decree Arts. 12-14); Art. 16(2)(2-2) support for generating training data from public-institution data; Art. 34(1)(2) — high-impact AI operators' explanation measures include an overview of training data. None of these data provisions carries a fine. — [datalaw.kr 인공지능기본법 조문 지도](https://datalaw.kr/ai-act/)
- **[ENACTED] (secondary)** Amendments: Act No. 21311 (promulgated 2026-01-20) added training-data provisions, some effective 2026-07-21. Act No. 22076 (promulgated 2026-10-20, **effective 2027-04-21**) deletes Art. 15(4)-(5) and creates **Art. 15-2: an integrated training-data provision system, with a duty on state bodies to link/provide data** (no decree yet). — [datalaw.kr](https://datalaw.kr/ai-act/)
- **[ENACTED]** Art. 31(1): an AI business operator providing a product/service using high-impact or generative AI must notify users in advance that it is AI-based; generated outputs must be labelled. The decree exempts cases where AI-based operation is obvious from the service name/screen text, and internal-only use. Fines exist only for three provisions — Art. 31(1) notice, Art. 36(1) domestic agent, Art. 40(3) non-compliance with suspension/correction orders — each up to KRW 30 million (Art. 43). — [Lexology(김·장) 시행령 초안 해설](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d3fa41a1-f09a-44da-8361-4e1eb7c61041); [datalaw.kr](https://datalaw.kr/ai-act/)
- **[ENACTED]/[COMMENTARY]** High-impact AI in finance: the Act names judgments/evaluations with significant effect on individual rights and duties such as **loan screening**; press reported (2025-11-26) that financial firms struggled because the decree draft gave no finer criteria. A legal column says a credit-scoring system that decides credit extension is regulated as high-impact AI. — [Daum 뉴스 전재 기사 2025-11-26](https://v.daum.net/v/20251126185048530); [법률신문 "대출심사와 고영향 AI"](https://www.lawtimes.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=217833)

**Market discourse**
- Vendor marketing has already attached to the public guideline (a synthetic-data vendor uses the v1.1 guideline and the public-data evaluation manual as the hook for its product). — [CUBIG blog 2026-09-21](https://cubig.ai/ko/articles/public-data-ai-friendly-guideline/)
- The Deputy PM/MSIT minister framed data as accounting for "80%" of AI performance improvement and the next 2-3 years as the "golden time" (political framing, not a measured figure). — [뉴시스 2026-05-28](https://mobile.newsis.com/view/NISX20260528_0003648050)

### Inferences
- The Korean official meaning of "AI-ready" is concrete and modest: **self-describing data** (schema + data dictionary), **lineage** (version, timestamps, change notes), **standard value formats** (ISO dates/currency codes, explicit nulls), **stated limits/bias**, and **machine-readable permission for AI use**. Every one of these translates directly to a small first-party dataset at near-zero cost; none requires a data platform.
- The programme's obligations run to public institutions (and are currently "reference", pending directive amendment). A private one-person app has **no compliance exposure** from the AI-Ready 공공데이터 programme itself. Its practical relevance is (a) vocabulary the market and officials now use, and (b) a free checklist.
- The AI 기본법 item with real bite for a small app is Art. 31(1) advance notice if a generative-AI feature is shown to users. A journal app that only uses an LLM for extraction with the result shown as "AI-read" text would most plausibly be handled by a plain on-screen notice — but whether extraction-only use counts as "providing a service using generative AI" was not confirmed in any source read here.
- The direction of travel (May 2026 plan; Art. 15-2 from April 2027) is toward **state-run training-data supply**, not toward private-sector data-readiness duties.

### Gaps
- **Budgets**: no 2025 or 2026 budget figure for AI-Ready public data was found in any source read (2026-01-06 and 2026-05-28 coverage contained none).
- **국가인공지능위원회 / 국가AI전략위원회**: no statement specifically on "AI-ready data" from this body was found; the 2026-05-28 announcement was attributed to a 데이터 관계장관회의.
- **TTA standard**: no TTA standard titled or scoped as "AI-ready data" was found. The guideline relies on international vocabularies (DCAT v3, Croissant). Whether TTA has a data-quality-for-AI standard was not checked directly on tta.or.kr.
- Primary text of the guideline (v1.0 and the claimed v1.1) and of the directive amendment was not opened; whether the directive has actually been amended as of Nov 2026 is unknown.
- Exact wording of AI 기본법 Art. 15 and the final enforcement-decree article numbers (one source cites decree Art. 22(4) for the transparency exemption, another maps Art. 31 to decree Art. 23) — the numbering conflict is unresolved; check law.go.kr.

## 2. Korean privacy: sending user records to an LLM, overseas transfer (PIPA §28-8), pseudonymisation, and MyData

### Takeaway
Using an overseas LLM API on users' records is treated by the regulator as an **overseas processing entrustment (국외 처리위탁)**: it can proceed without separate consent under PIPA Art. 28-8(1)(3) **only if** the processing is necessary to perform the contract with the data subject **and** the Art. 28-8(2) items are disclosed in the privacy policy (or notified) — the PIPC issued a warning in June 2024 to a call-recording/summary service precisely for omitting that disclosure. Bringing brokerage transaction data into a third-party app through financial MyData requires a 본인신용정보관리업 licence (minimum capital KRW 500 million), which is out of reach for a one-person product; the 2026 expansion of the PIPA self-transfer right mainly obliges large data holders to let users download their own data.

### Cited Findings

**PIPA overseas-transfer structure**
- **[ENACTED]** Art. 28-8 permits overseas transfer on one of: (1) separate consent of the data subject; (2) special provision in a law/treaty; (3) processing entrustment or storage **necessary for concluding and performing a contract with the data subject**; (4) certification designated by the PIPC; (5) adequacy recognition of the destination. The transfer basis (or the consent content) must be published in the privacy policy (Enforcement Decree Art. 31(1)(2)); contracts contrary to PIPA are prohibited (Art. 28-8(5)); protective measures are required (Decree Art. 29-10); onward transfer to a third country is covered mutatis mutandis (Art. 28-11). — [개인정보보호위원회 국외이전 제도 안내](https://pipc.go.kr/np/default/page.do?mCode=D060040010)
- **[COMMENTARY]** Item (3) has two layers: a substantive requirement (necessary for performing the contract *with the data subject*) and a procedural one (policy disclosure or notice). Writing it in the policy only satisfies the second layer. Art. 28-8(2) items to disclose: personal-data items transferred; destination country, timing and method; recipient; purpose and retention period; method, procedure and effect of refusal. — [datalaw.kr, 이현섭 변호사, 2026-10-22 (rev. 2026-11-21)](https://datalaw.kr/posts/ai-input-third-party-overseas-transfer/)
- **[COMMENTARY]** Adequacy (item 5) currently covers EU/EEA and is unusable for flows to US servers; item (4) requires checking the overseas AI vendor's certification separately. — [datalaw.kr](https://datalaw.kr/posts/ai-input-third-party-overseas-transfer/)

**Enforcement precedent on overseas LLM processing**
- **[ENACTED — regulator decision, via secondary]** PIPC Decision No. 2024-010-184 (2024-06-12, "주요 AI 서비스 사전 실태점검 결과"): a service that provided call recording/summarisation sent call text and audio to overseas servers; the PIPC characterised this as **overseas processing entrustment** and found the Art. 28-8(2) items had not been disclosed in the privacy policy or notified. Outcome: **warning** (the respondent had corrected its policy on 2024-05-02). The PIPC's generative-AI guidebook (p. 25) describes this as failing to meet the item-(3) legal basis. The author cautions this is a pre-inspection warning and not a broad precedent, and that the decision did not rule on whether item (3) covers the *other party* to the call. — [datalaw.kr](https://datalaw.kr/posts/ai-input-third-party-overseas-transfer/)

**PIPC generative-AI guidebook (2025-08-06) [GUIDELINE]**
- 「생성형 AI 개발·활용을 위한 개인정보 처리 안내서」 sets criteria over four lifecycle stages (purpose setting → strategy → training/development → deployment/management) and distinguishes three types: **service-type LLM (e.g. API integration)**, off-the-shelf/open-source LLM, and self-developed models. — [바이라인네트워크 2025-08-06](https://byline.network/2025/08/6-277/); [Daum 뉴스 전재 기사 2025-08-06](https://v.daum.net/v/20250806130146966)
- For external APIs: user inputs may go to overseas servers, so safety should be secured **through contract or terms**; purpose and scope of personal data must be fixed at the purpose-setting stage (an AI digital-textbook service was corrected for unclear purpose of learning-history data). If user prompts are used for training, consider relevance to performance improvement, user foreseeability, and whether an **opt-out** is offered. Pseudonymisation/anonymisation and other PETs are recommended at the training stage. After deployment: transparent privacy-policy disclosure and opt-out. Also recommended: privacy-by-design, impact assessment, CPO involvement from planning, audit logs. — [바이라인네트워크 2025-08-06](https://byline.network/2025/08/6-277/); [디지털데일리 2025-08-06](https://www.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2025080611120338544)
- System-level measures summarised by a law firm: access control when linking external APIs, input/output filters, extra safeguards for RAG / external DBs (from search summary). — [법무법인 세종 뉴스레터](https://shinkim.com/kor/media/newsletter/2941?page=46)
- **[GUIDELINE]** 2026-05-19: the PIPC published a user-facing guide on personal-data protection when using generative-AI services; it warns that entering third parties' information risks transfer outside "without prior consent or legal basis" but does not name the provision. A privacy-policy drafting guide (2026-04) gives an example entry of transferred items as "all data entered in the chat window". — [Daum 2026-05-19](https://v.daum.net/v/20260519222300715); [법률신문 해설](https://www.lawtimes.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=222055); characterisation of the gap from [datalaw.kr](https://datalaw.kr/posts/ai-input-third-party-overseas-transfer/)
- **[COMMENTARY]** A law-firm column (2026-08-13) stresses that overseas-run or overseas-hosted generative AI means inputs may be processed abroad; that PIPA requires a legal basis (separate consent, certification, etc.) with disclosure of purpose, items, country, retention and refusal method; that multi-tier sub-processing can cause onward transfers the company does not know about; and recommends confirming storage location, sub-processors and training use before contracting and re-checking periodically. It notes the AI 기본법 does not regulate overseas transfer — that remains PIPA. — [ZDNet Korea 기고 (법무법인 태평양) 2026-08-13](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20260813164711)
- **[COMMENTARY]** Practical checklist from the same practitioner source: record which Art. 28-8 limb each data category relies on; exclude or de-identify inputs with no basis; note that deleting name/contact does not de-identify if content plus role/affiliation still identifies the person; check enterprise terms for processor clause, **no-training**, sub-processor list and Decree Art. 29-10 safeguards. An enterprise DPA presupposes an entrustment structure but does not itself satisfy the item-(3) substantive requirement. — [datalaw.kr](https://datalaw.kr/posts/ai-input-third-party-overseas-transfer/)

**Proposed change affecting AI training on personal data**
- **[PLAN]** 2026-05-28: government said it would quickly pursue a PIPA amendment allowing original data to be used for **public-interest** AI training subject to safety, and a 공공데이터법 amendment for pseudonymisation. Not enacted in any source read. — [뉴시스 2026-05-28](https://mobile.newsis.com/view/NISX20260528_0003648050)

**MyData — financial (신용정보법)**
- **[ENACTED]** Financial MyData 2.0: 27 MyData operators began service on **2025-06-19** (others to follow). Changes: users pick a sector (bank/insurance/securities) and retrieve all holdings at once; the 50-institution connection cap was removed; asset-query consent cut from two steps to one; membership validity selectable in 1-year units up to **5 years**; periodic transmission stops after **6 months** without login and user data is deleted after **1 year** without login. Remaining items targeted for H2 2025. — [금융위원회 보도자료](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/84780)
- **[ENACTED]** Receiving a user's financial data through this channel requires a **본인신용정보관리업 licence**: minimum capital **KRW 500 million**, plus physical/security facilities (hacking prevention, network separation), business-plan soundness, major-shareholder fitness, officer fitness and expertise. — [네플라 위키 (법률 해설)](https://www.nepla.ai/wiki/it-정보-방송통신/개인정보-위치정보-신용정보/늘어나는-마이데이터-사업자위한-마이데이터-사업-관련-규제-및-법령-rv5n72m158zm); [금융감독원 허가설명회 자료(2020)](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/허가설명회-금융감독원.pdf)
- Licensed operators collect via open API rather than screen scraping (reported at the 2021 licensing round; from a search-engine summary that did not make clear which of the returned pages carried the statement — low confidence on the exact source, and no statutory provision banning scraping was retrieved). — [딜사이트 기사](https://dealsite.co.kr/articles/70338); [지평 뉴스레터](https://jipyong.com/kr/board/news_view.php?nownum=2163&seq=9553)

**MyData — all sectors (PIPA 전송요구권)**
- **[ENACTED]** The PIPA transmission-request right has applied since **2025-03-13**, initially centred on medical and telecom. A decree amendment extending the **self-transfer (본인전송) right to all sectors** passed the Cabinet on **2026-02-10**. — [전자신문 2026-02-10](https://www.etnews.com/20260210000384); [바이라인네트워크 2026-02-10](https://byline.network/2026/02/10-613/)
- **[ENACTED, effective date partly uncertain]** Obligated "본인대상정보전송자": controllers with average revenue above **KRW 180 billion** and either ≥1 million data subjects or ≥50,000 subjects' sensitive/unique-identifier data, plus public-system operators; **SMEs are excluded**. Transferable data: data processed on consent, for contract, or under law; excludable: separately generated/derived data (e.g. risk classifications, statistics), data infringing third parties' rights, trade secrets. Method: API in principle; scraping only for pre-agreed, vetted agents; encrypted download of what is viewable on the website is also allowed. Grace: 6 months after promulgation for public-system operators and third-party transferors, **1 year** for large private controllers. Press said it takes effect "in August" 2026. — [ZDNet Korea 2026-02-16](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20260216123214)
- **[PLAN]** Third-party transfer to be widened in 2026 to energy, education, employment, culture/leisure via working groups; 개인정보관리 전문기관 designation briefings from March 2026; the PIPC began checking public institutions' readiness in April 2026. — [ZDNet Korea 2026-02-16](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20260216123214); [머니투데이 2026-04-29](https://www.mt.co.kr/tech/2026/04/29/2026042909253026337)
- Industry concern reported: leak risk and use of domestic consumer data by foreign (Chinese) platforms. — [ZDNet Korea 2026-02-16](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20260216123214)

### Inferences
- For the app's own users, the data subject **is** the contracting party, so Art. 28-8(1)(3) is the natural basis for an LLM extraction/reading feature — provided the feature is genuinely part of the service the user signed up for, and the privacy policy lists the Art. 28-8(2) items (vendor as overseas processor, country, items, purpose, retention, how to refuse). The June 2024 decision shows the regulator's focus is the **disclosure**, and that the sanction for a first, corrected omission was a warning. If AI use is optional or goes beyond what the service needs, separate consent (item 1) is the safer limb. This is an inference from commentary, not a ruling on this fact pattern.
- A trade journal's free-text reasons can contain third parties (family, a friend who gave a tip, an employer). Those people are not contracting parties, which is exactly the unresolved zone the commentary identifies. Masking before text leaves the device reduces, but by the commentary's own caveat does not eliminate, that exposure.
- Recording **which consent/notice version was in force when each record was collected** is the cheapest way to keep future AI use lawful: the guidebook's tests for reusing user inputs (foreseeability, relevance, opt-out) can only be answered per record if scope was recorded at collection.
- Pseudonymisation (가명처리) is the tool for *training or statistics without consent*; it does not address the more likely small-product case (per-user inference on that user's own data), where purpose limitation and transfer disclosure matter more.
- MyData routes: (a) financial MyData is closed to a one-person product (licence, capital, security infrastructure); (b) the PIPA self-transfer right helps only insofar as the user's broker is an obligated holder and offers a download — the user then imports the file. The realistic "bring your own brokerage data" path for a micro-product therefore remains **user-mediated import** (file export, capture, pasted text), which also keeps the product outside the 신용정보법 licensing perimeter. Whether an app that merely accepts a user-uploaded export could be regarded as conducting 본인신용정보관리업 was not addressed in any source read.

### Gaps
- Exact statutory wording of Art. 28-8(1)(3) and (2), Art. 28-9 (suspension order) and the penalty provisions was not retrieved from law.go.kr; the PIPC page read does not reproduce them.
- The PIPC generative-AI guidebook PDF itself (2025-08) was not opened; page-level citations (e.g. p. 25) come from a practitioner's article.
- Whether securities firms' transaction histories fall under the PIPA all-sector self-transfer regime or remain exclusively under 신용정보법 — not stated in any source read.
- Actual implementation status of the all-sector self-transfer decree as of Nov 2026 (one search summary mentions an effective date of 20 August 2026; unverified).
- 「공개된 개인정보 처리 안내서」 (2024) and the 가명정보 처리 가이드라인 were not individually researched; the former concerns scraping publicly available data for model training and appears of low relevance to first-party journal data.
- Whether 본인신용정보관리업 capital and licensing requirements have changed since the 2020-2021 sources.

## 3. Small-product practice: keeping future AI use possible at low cost; evidence on LLMs over personal-finance/journal records; the investment-advice line

### Takeaway
Credible, concrete guidance converges on cheap habits rather than infrastructure — append-only records, explicit schema and versioning, timestamps and provenance, originals preserved, licence/consent scope stored with the data — which is also what the Korean public guideline asks of agencies. Evidence on LLMs reading users' financial records is early and mostly preprint, but points to two specific risks for commentary features: models side with the person asking, and "personalised" investment output collapses onto one or two inputs; under Korean law individualised, interactive investment advice is reserved to registered 투자자문업자.

### Cited Findings

**Data-design practices**
- The MOIS/NIA guideline's mandatory checklist (secondary) is a usable minimal spec: open standard format; structure that can be auto-processed; a **data dictionary** with variable definitions, units, calculation basis and code values; uniform date/code notation; a consistent rule for missing/special values; quality results recorded in metadata; **synthetic data and auto-generated annotations clearly distinguished** from original data; de-identification of personal data; licence/permitted-use scope stated in machine-readable form; a named responsible party and an error-report channel. — [PyTorch KR summary of the guideline](https://discuss.pytorch.kr/t/v1-0-pdf-markdown-70p/10084)
- Same source: lineage metadata (version, issued/modified timestamps, version notes with concrete changes) is mandatory, and documents must be updated with a change log whenever data changes. — [PyTorch KR summary](https://discuss.pytorch.kr/t/v1-0-pdf-markdown-70p/10084)
- General engineering write-ups recommend append-only events that are never updated or deleted, designing events for schema evolution from day one (start with JSON Schema, move to Avro/Protobuf only at scale), including only what consumers need, retaining original provenance through transformations, and keeping sensitive data out of events or encrypting it. These are generic tutorial posts from an observability vendor's blog, not evidence-based studies. — [OneUptime: event store design (2026-01-30)](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-01-30-event-store-design/view); [OneUptime: event schema design (2026-01-30)](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-01-30-event-schema-design/view)
- PIPC guidebook: fix purpose and data scope first; secure external-API safety by contract/terms; input and output filters; opt-out and transparent policy after deployment; audit logs. — [바이라인네트워크 2025-08-06](https://byline.network/2025/08/6-277/); [법무법인 세종 뉴스레터](https://shinkim.com/kor/media/newsletter/2941?page=46)
- Practitioner caveat on masking: removing names and contact details does not make text non-personal if content plus role/affiliation still identifies someone. — [datalaw.kr 2026-10-22](https://datalaw.kr/posts/ai-input-third-party-overseas-transfer/)
- Export: under the 2026 self-transfer rules, acceptable delivery includes API or an encrypted download of what the user can already view; derived/analysed data may be excluded from the transfer duty. (Applies to large controllers; cited here as the regulator's notion of an adequate export.) — [ZDNet Korea 2026-02-16](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20260216123214)

**Evidence on LLMs over financial/personal records**
- Ross & Lo (arXiv 2604.23837, submitted 2026-04-26, preprint): frontier LLMs giving investment advice show "systematic heuristic collapse" — "a systematic reduction of complex, multi-factor decisions to a small number of dominant inputs"; allocations are "largely determined by self-reported risk tolerance"; web search "partially attenuates heuristic collapse but does not resolve it"; "deploying LLMs as advisors requires auditing input sensitivity, not just output quality." (Abstract only; no figures available.) — [arXiv:2604.23837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23837)
- A 2026 preprint studies modes of sycophancy in agentic financial tasks — models yielding to user pushback/contradiction — and tests mitigations such as input filtering (from search summary; not read in full). — [arXiv:2604.24668](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24668)
- An unpublished draft applying social-sycophancy criteria to interpersonal financial disputes reports all four tested models favoured the advice-seeker's perspective (preliminary draft; from search summary). — [Marley-Payne draft](https://jackmarleypayne.com/assets/pdfs/marley-payne-fin-conflict.pdf)
- Counter-evidence: a preregistered experiment across seven LLMs and twelve investment scenarios found fraud warnings held up under investor pressure; the authors argue against blanket restriction of AI advisory tools (preprint; from search summary). — [arXiv:2604.20652](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20652)
- Fieberg, Hornuf, Meiler & Streich (SSRN, Jan 2025) test whether LLMs can generate suitable financial advice and examine discrimination in outputs (findings not retrieved). — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388904444_Using_Large_Language_Models_for_Financial_Advice)
- ESMA/ILB/Turing report (June 2025) on LLMs in finance warns that poorly vetted AI tools can produce inaccurate or misleading advice leading to losses (from search summary). — [ESMA report PDF](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/LLMs_in_finance_-_ILB_ESMA_Turing_Report.pdf)

**Regulatory line: AI commentary on a user's trades (자본시장법)**
- **[ENACTED — regulator interpretation]** FSC/FSS: responding to individual customers' questions or counselling 1:1 to give individualised investment advice is **투자자문행위 and is prohibited to 유사투자자문업자**; a 유사투자자문업자 may only give non-individualised advice to unspecified many through one-way channels; managing under delegated discretion is 투자일임업. — [금융규제·법령해석포털 회신](https://better.fsc.go.kr/fsc_new/replyCase/LawreqDetail.do?stNo=11&muNo=85&muGpNo=75&lawreqIdx=3505); [금융위·금감원 보도참고](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/81575?srchCtgry=&curPage=&srchKey=&srchText=&srchBeginDt=&srchEndDt=)
- **[COMMENTARY]** A startup-oriented explainer classifies stock recommendations reflecting a customer's profile as 투자자문업, order execution as 투자일임업, order routing as 투자중개업 (blog-grade source). — [K-Startbiz blog](https://blog.k-startbiz.org/%EB%B2%95%EC%9D%B8%EC%84%A4%EB%A6%BD/ai-%ED%88%AC%EC%9E%90-%EC%84%9C%EB%B9%84%EC%8A%A4-%EB%AC%B4%EC%97%87%EB%B6%80%ED%84%B0-%EC%A4%80%EB%B9%84%ED%95%B4%EC%95%BC-%ED%95%98%EB%82%98/)

### Inferences
- A defensible minimal definition of "AI-ready" for a one-person journal product, assembled from the sources above: (1) every record has an ISO-8601 timestamp, a stable ID and a **source/provenance tag** (user-typed vs imported file vs machine-extracted); (2) raw answers and raw free text are stored verbatim and never overwritten by derived values; (3) derived or model-generated fields are stored separately and labelled as such (the guideline's "distinguish auto-generated annotations" rule); (4) questionnaire and schema **versions** are recorded with each record; (5) the consent/notice version and permitted-use scope in force at collection is recorded; (6) a one-page data dictionary exists; (7) a complete user export in an open format (JSON/CSV) exists. Items 1-5 are columns, 6 is a document, 7 doubles as the PIPA portability/withdrawal deliverable. None needs embeddings, a vector store, a feature store or a pipeline.
- The research risks map onto product design, not data design: an LLM that *comments* on a user's trades in response to that user's own records is an interactive, individualised channel — the shape Korean regulators assign to registered 투자자문업 when the content is investment judgement. Extraction-only use (reading a fill confirmation into structured fields) and descriptive mirroring of the user's own past statements stay much further from that line than any forward-looking evaluation of a holding or trade. No FSC interpretation specific to AI chat/commentary was found, so this is inference by analogy from the human 1:1 rule.
- Heuristic collapse and advice-seeker bias both argue that if AI commentary is ever added, the test to run first is **input-sensitivity and agreement-bias evaluation on the app's own records**, which presupposes exactly the cheap properties above (raw text kept, provenance known) and nothing more.

### Gaps
- No rigorous, evidence-based study was found on event/record design for *small* consumer products with future LLM use in mind; the engineering sources located are generic tutorials. Recommendations in this section are therefore assembled from regulator guidance plus general practice, not from measured outcomes.
- No study was found of LLM features in **journaling** or trade-journal apps specifically (benefit or harm). The finance papers concern advice/allocation tasks, mostly as 2026 preprints not yet peer reviewed.
- No FSC/FSS interpretation, no-action letter or enforcement case specifically about AI-generated commentary on a user's own trades was found (search of secondary sources only; better.fsc.go.kr was not queried directly for "AI").
- EDPB/ICO guidance was not consulted; no point arose that required it.
- Details (models, sample sizes, effect sizes) of the cited preprints were not retrieved beyond abstracts/snippets.

## 4. Counter-view: is "AI-ready data" premature or over-sold for small teams?

### Takeaway
No source was found arguing outright that "AI-ready data" is over-sold for small teams, but the mainstream sources themselves undercut any up-front programme: Gartner says data cannot be made AI-ready in general or in advance of a use case, and the leading evals practitioners say to look at real data and failures before building tooling. Combined with the Korean guideline's own admission that it does not guarantee immediate AI usability, the supportable position is "keep cheap optionality, defer everything use-case-specific".

### Cited Findings
- Gartner (press release 2025-02-26): predicts organisations will abandon 60% of AI projects unsupported by AI-ready data through 2026 (from search snippet; the page returned HTTP 403 when fetched, so the exact wording and the survey base were not verified). — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-26-lack-of-ai-ready-data-puts-ai-projects-at-risk)
- Gartner's explainer states there is no way to make data AI-ready in general or in advance, and that "high-quality" data by traditional data-quality standards does not equate to AI-ready data — i.e. readiness is defined against a specific use case (from search snippet; page returned 403 on fetch). — [Gartner: What is AI-ready data](https://gartner.com/en/articles/ai-ready-data)
- A 2026 Gartner piece (Japanese syndication, 2026-09-11) says AI-ready data cannot be achieved through one-time data preparation and that new approaches beyond conventional "AI-ready data" must be understood (from search snippet). — [@IT Gartner Insights Pickup 2026-09-11](https://atmarkit.itmedia.co.jp/ait/articles/2609/11/news006.html)
- TechTarget reports that only 32% of organisations with AI initiatives had an AI data-readiness process (from search snippet; survey source/date not verified). — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchenterpriseai/feature/AI-ready-data-needs-its-own-set-of-rules-experts-say)
- Vendor framing: a data-catalogue vendor's summary of Gartner notes AI-ready data is often misread as simply "having enough data" (vendor source with commercial interest). — [Alation blog 2024-09-24](https://www.alation.com/blog/gartner-report-ai-ready-data-drives-success/)
- Evals-first practitioners: Hamel Husain argues teams should look at data first to understand failures and decide which evals to write; a rubric "is an output of error analysis, not a prerequisite for it"; "Automating Too Much" is listed as a pitfall; most effort should go to understanding failures rather than building automated checks. — [Arize write-up of Husain talk](https://arize.com/blog/rise-of-the-ai-engineer-why-ai-evals-fail-before-the-evaluation-begins.md); [Husain slides, "The Revenge of the Data Scientist"](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/revenge/images/The_Revenge_of_the_Data_Scientist.pdf); [Simon Willison's notes on Husain](https://simonwillison.net/tags/hamel-husain/)
- Lightweight tooling suffices early: one team spent a few hours building a simple custom data viewer with an AI assistant rather than adopting generic tools. — [The Pragmatic Engineer: evals](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/evals)
- The Korean guideline itself states it does not guarantee immediate AI usability of all data and that unstructured-data criteria are not yet adequately covered (secondary). — [PyTorch KR summary](https://discuss.pytorch.kr/t/v1-0-pdf-markdown-70p/10084)
- A vendor selling into the public AI-ready programme concedes no tool can guarantee an evaluation score — while using the guideline as a sales hook. — [CUBIG blog 2026-09-21](https://cubig.ai/ko/articles/public-data-ai-friendly-guideline/)
- Personalisation evidence cuts against "more data → better AI output": current frontier models' investment allocations are driven mostly by one self-reported field regardless of the rest of the profile. — [arXiv:2604.23837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23837)

### Inferences
- The strongest argument against an "AI-readiness programme" for a one-person product comes from the pro-readiness camp's own definition: readiness is relative to a use case, so with **no committed AI use case there is nothing to be ready for** beyond not destroying information (raw text, timestamps, provenance, consent scope). Work beyond that — embeddings, chunking, labelled sets, pipelines — is speculative and likely to be redone once a use case exists.
- For a closed beta with few users, data volume is far below what any training or fine-tuning would need; the only realistic AI use is per-user inference in context, for which modern models need legible records, not a prepared dataset. (Reasoned inference; no source quantified a threshold.)
- Sequencing that the evidence supports: (1) preserve optionality now at column-level cost; (2) when a specific feature is proposed, hand-review real records and write a small eval first; (3) only then build whatever preparation that feature needs; (4) legal gating (Art. 28-8 disclosure, AI-notice, advice line) is a prerequisite of step 3, not of step 1.
- The Korean discourse is supply-side (government releasing data for model builders and startups). A product whose asset is users' private behavioural records gains little from that supply and should not read the national slogan as a signal to invest.

### Gaps
- No direct, named expert critique was found saying "AI-ready data is over-hyped" or "premature for small teams"; the counter-view here is constructed from Gartner's use-case-specific definition and the evals-first literature. A report should present it as an inference, not as a quoted position.
- Gartner pages returned HTTP 403; the 60% prediction and the "cannot be made AI-ready in advance" statement rest on search-engine snippets and should be verified before being quoted.
- No evidence was found on minimum data volumes for useful per-user LLM features, or on cost/benefit of retrofitting provenance and consent metadata later versus capturing it at collection.
- No Korean-language critique of the AI-Ready 공공데이터 programme (e.g. from civil-society or academic commentators) was searched for or found.
